# Product descriptions to paste into wp-admin

34 products, every one of which currently has an empty description.

**How these were written.** Each draft restates only what is already in the
product's own title, category and price. Nothing here invents a material, a
measurement, a warranty or a country of origin — where a spec would obviously
help and the data does not have it, there is a `CHECK` note asking you for it
instead of a guess. Descriptions that state facts the product does not have are
worse than no description: they produce returns, disputes and chargebacks.

**Two fields per product.** WooCommerce has both:

- **Short description** — the paragraph beside the price on the product page. One
  or two lines. This is also what search engines most often lift as the snippet.
- **Description** — the long tab lower down. Three or four lines is plenty; a wall
  of text is not read.

**What is deliberately not in these.** Delivery times, the returns window and the
payment methods are already printed on every product page by the storefront
itself, pulled from your wp-admin settings. Repeating them in each description
would mean 34 copies of a promise you would then have to edit in 34 places when
it changes. Leave that to the template.

# Before you publish: three things this exercise turned up

## 1. One price is almost certainly wrong

**Nike Shox R4 (ID 543) is listed at UGX 1,840,367.** Every other shoe you sell
is between UGX 110,000 and 185,000. That is not a premium, it is a tenfold
difference, and it looks like a mis-typed digit or a bad currency conversion.

This costs you more than one sale. A shopper who sees it assumes your prices
cannot be trusted and stops believing the other thirty-three.

## 2. Three products are in the wrong department

- **ASICS Gel-Kayano 14 (521)** — running shoes filed under **Hoodies**
- **Air pump (1329)** — filed under **Kids**
- **Cotton striped men's tee (1238)** — filed under **Kids**

These are invisible to anyone browsing the department they belong to, and they
turn up as nonsense in the mega menu. Fixing them in wp-admin takes a minute each
and needs no code.

## 3. Branded listings — a decision only you can make

Many of these are branded goods: Hublot, Nike, Adidas, ASICS, Manchester City,
Arsenal. Several are priced far below what the genuine article costs — a Hublot
watch at UGX 180,000 is the clearest case.

I have written these descriptions to describe **what the item is and does**
without asserting that it is manufactured by or licensed from the brand named. I
have not written "authentic", "official" or "genuine" anywhere, and you should
not add those words unless the goods really are.

This is not me being squeamish. Copy that explicitly claims authenticity for
goods that are not genuine is what turns a customer complaint into a refund
demand, a payment-processor dispute, or a trademark takedown of the shop. The
same descriptions are perfectly safe as long as they do not make that claim.

If these **are** authentic and you have supplier documentation, tell me and I
will rewrite them to say so plainly — an authenticity guarantee is a strong
selling point and worth stating loudly.

## And the pattern behind almost every CHECK note

Look at how many of the notes above are asking for the same thing: **sizes**.

Fourteen of these products are shoes or clothing, and not one lists the sizes it
comes in. That is the single most common reason a fashion order is abandoned and
the single most common reason one is returned. No description, however well
written, substitutes for it.

If you do one thing from this file before writing a word of copy, add sizes to
the fourteen footwear and clothing listings.
